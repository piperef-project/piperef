Lessons learned: rework and safety on site

Common ways piping goes wrong between the design office and start-up, and how to stop them happening on your project. Below them, real stories shared by engineers.

About these cases. The cases in the first section are typical, composite examples written to teach. They are based on problems that recur across the industry and do not describe any specific project, company or person. Stories in the second section are submitted by engineers, anonymised and reviewed before they appear.

Typical cases 12

ReworkTypical case

Pump suction rerouted after vendor data changed

What happened
A pump suction was routed and isometrics issued using the preliminary vendor drawing. The certified drawing moved the suction nozzle and changed its size.
Impact
Spools were already fabricated. The suction had to be rerouted, new spools made, and the first ones scrapped.
How to prevent it
  • Mark piping near equipment as HOLD until certified vendor data is received.
  • Track vendor drawing status for every critical equipment item.
  • Avoid issuing pump and compressor isometrics for fabrication on preliminary data.
ReworkTypical case

Supports missing on a hydrotest of a gas line

What happened
A large-bore gas line was designed and supported for gas only. During hydrotest, the water weight was several times the operating weight.
Impact
Supports and the rack beam were overloaded; the test was stopped and temporary supports added.
How to prevent it
  • List lines that need temporary supports or a different test medium in the test pack.
  • Lock spring supports before hydrotest.
  • Ask stress and civil to check test weight for large-bore gas lines.
SafetyTypical case

Test blind left in after reinstatement

What happened
A spade installed to isolate a control valve during hydrotest was not removed afterwards. It was not recorded on the reinstatement list.
Impact
At start-up the line had no flow, causing an upset and a delay while the system was depressurised to remove it.
How to prevent it
  • Number and log every temporary blind and spade in the test pack.
  • Use brightly painted blinds with long handles.
  • Close out the blind list with a signed walkdown before commissioning.
SafetyTypical case

Small-bore drain cracked by vibration

What happened
A 3/4" drain with a valve cantilevered from a reciprocating compressor discharge line had no bracing.
Impact
Vibration fatigue cracked the weld at the branch, causing a gas leak and an unplanned shutdown.
How to prevent it
  • Keep small-bore connections near vibrating equipment short and braced to the main pipe.
  • Avoid heavy valves on unsupported small branches.
  • Include small-bore checks in vibration surveys and model reviews.
ReworkTypical case

Insulation not modelled, clashes on site

What happened
Lines were modelled without insulation thickness on a congested rack. Clash checks looked clean.
Impact
On site, insulated lines clashed with steel and other lines; several had to be rerouted and supports moved.
How to prevent it
  • Model insulation from the line list as an attribute, not a note.
  • Run soft-clash checks that include insulation and access clearances.
  • Check attributes in model reviews, not just geometry.
ReworkTypical case

Wrong reducer orientation at a pump suction

What happened
An eccentric reducer at a pump suction was fitted flat side down to match the pipe rack bottom-of-pipe convention.
Impact
A vapour pocket formed at the pump inlet, causing cavitation and poor performance until the reducer was turned.
How to prevent it
  • Show FOT / FOB clearly on isometrics.
  • Check reducer orientation at every pump suction during iso checking and erection.
  • Remember: pump suction flat on top; rack and sleeper runs flat on bottom.
SafetyTypical case

Relief line with a pocket

What happened
A relief discharge line was routed down and then up to reach the flare header to avoid a structure.
Impact
Liquid collected in the low point. When the valve lifted, the slug loaded the supports and the flare header.
How to prevent it
  • Route relief and flare lines without pockets, sloping to the header or knock-out drum.
  • Enter flare headers from the top.
  • Review relief routing as a separate item in model reviews.
ReworkTypical case

Valve unreachable for operation

What happened
An isolation valve was placed at about 3 m above grade near a column, with no platform or chain wheel.
Impact
Operators could not reach it during commissioning; a platform extension had to be added.
How to prevent it
  • Check valve operating heights against the access standard in every model review.
  • Mark operate-in-emergency valves for priority access.
  • Use the Valve access page to check heights early.
SafetyTypical case

Flange leak at start-up from reused gasket

What happened
A flange opened during commissioning was closed again using the same spiral wound gasket.
Impact
The joint leaked hydrocarbon at temperature and had to be isolated and remade.
How to prevent it
  • Never reuse gaskets. Keep spares on site.
  • Tag critical joints and record assembly per a controlled procedure.
  • Use calibrated tools and a cross pattern for tightening.
ReworkTypical case

Material mix-up in alloy piping

What happened
A carbon steel elbow was installed in a Cr-Mo high-temperature line. Both looked the same.
Impact
PMI found it before start-up, but the spool had to be cut out, replaced and heat treated again.
How to prevent it
  • Do PMI on alloy components and welds.
  • Segregate and colour-code alloy material in stores.
  • Check material certificates against the BOM before fabrication.
SafetyTypical case

Pneumatic test without exclusion zone

What happened
A section was tested with air because water could not be used, but the test area was not barricaded.
Impact
A near miss: a fitting failed and fragments travelled several metres. Nobody was hurt because the area happened to be empty.
How to prevent it
  • Treat pneumatic tests as high-energy tests needing an approved procedure.
  • Set and enforce an exclusion zone.
  • Pressurise in stages with holds and relief protection on the test source.
ReworkTypical case

Tie-in on a live plant without verified existing data

What happened
A brownfield tie-in was designed from old drawings. The existing line had been modified years earlier.
Impact
The new spool did not fit at the shutdown; time was lost making a field spool during a tight shutdown window.
How to prevent it
  • Survey or laser-scan existing tie-in points before design.
  • Verify sizes, ratings, wall thickness and materials on site.
  • Plan field-fit welds with extra length for brownfield tie-ins.
