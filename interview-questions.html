Piping basics

FresherWhat is the difference between a pipe and a tube?

Pipe is specified by nominal size (NPS or DN) and schedule, and its OD is fixed for each size; it is used to carry fluids. Tube is specified by its actual OD and wall thickness and is used where exact dimensions matter, such as heat exchangers, instrument lines and boilers.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat does NPS mean, and when does the OD equal the nominal size?

NPS (nominal pipe size) is a size name. From NPS 1/8 to NPS 12 the OD is larger than the number (NPS 6 has an OD of 168.3 mm). From NPS 14 upwards the OD in inches equals the NPS. The OD stays the same for every schedule; only the wall thickness and bore change.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is a pipe schedule?

A schedule (Sch 10, 40, 80, 160 and so on) is a wall thickness series in ASME B36.10M for carbon and alloy steel and B36.19M for stainless steel (the S schedules). For one NPS, a higher schedule means a thicker wall and a smaller bore.

Link to this answer
FresherHow do you convert NPS to DN?

For most sizes multiply the NPS by 25 (NPS 2 = DN 50, NPS 6 = DN 150, NPS 24 = DN 600). Watch the small sizes: NPS 1/2 = DN 15, 3/4 = DN 20, 1-1/4 = DN 32, 1-1/2 = DN 40, 2-1/2 = DN 65, 3-1/2 = DN 90.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat are the main piping codes used in plants?

ASME B31.3 for process piping in refineries and chemical plants, B31.1 for power piping, B31.4 for liquid pipelines, B31.8 for gas transmission and distribution, and B31.9 for building services. Component standards such as B16.5 (flanges), B16.9 (butt-weld fittings) and B16.11 (forged fittings) sit under these codes.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is the difference between a code, a standard and a specification?

A code (for example B31.3) sets the minimum rules for safe design, fabrication, inspection and testing and is often made legally binding. A standard defines products and dimensions (for example B16.5 flanges). A specification is the project or client document that selects and adds requirements, such as the piping material specification.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat documents does a piping designer use as inputs?

P&IDs, the plot plan, equipment layouts and datasheets, vendor drawings, the piping material specification (piping classes), the line list, design basis and layout philosophy, plus inputs from civil, structural, instrument and electrical disciplines.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is a P&ID?

A piping and instrumentation diagram shows every line, valve, instrument, control loop and item of equipment in a unit, with line numbers, sizes, piping classes and special requirements. It is not to scale. Piping layout must follow the P&ID exactly, including notes such as slope, no pockets or straight lengths.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is a line list?

A line list (line designation table) gives, for each line number, the size, fluid, piping class, design and operating pressure and temperature, test pressure and medium, insulation, tracing, painting and the P&ID reference. It is the main data source for isometric title blocks.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat does a line number usually contain?

Typically the size, fluid or service code, a sequence number, the piping class and the insulation code, for example 6"-P-1001-A1A-H. The exact format is defined by the project numbering procedure.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is a piping class?

A piping class (pipe spec) is a sheet in the piping material specification that fixes, for a pressure–temperature–fluid range, the materials, flange rating and facing, wall thicknesses by size, fitting types, valve types, branch table, gaskets and bolting. Designers must only use components listed in the line’s class.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is design pressure and design temperature?

The most severe coincident pressure and temperature expected in service, including upsets, used to size the wall thickness and choose the flange rating. They are set by process and are higher than normal operating conditions.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is the difference between a header and a branch?

The header is the main run that carries flow to or from several users; branches come off the header to individual equipment or users. Branch connection type (tee, olet, stub-in) is chosen from the branch table in the piping class.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhy is NPS 2-1/2, 3-1/2 and 5 avoided in many plants?

They are non-preferred sizes: fittings, valves and flanges are less available and more expensive, and they add stock items. Most specifications skip them except at equipment connections where the vendor supplies that size.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is the minimum line size normally used in process plants, and why?

Usually NPS 3/4 or NPS 1 for process lines in the rack and NPS 2 for pipe on sleepers or racks, because small bore pipe is easily damaged and needs close support spacing. Small bore below this is kept short and local to equipment or instruments.

Link to this answer

Pipe, fittings and flanges

FresherWhat are the common types of pipe ends?

Plain end (PE) for socket weld or slip-on joints, bevelled end (BE) for butt welding, and threaded end (TE) for screwed joints. Large and higher-pressure lines are butt-welded; small bore is usually socket-welded or threaded depending on the class.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is the difference between seamless and welded pipe?

Seamless pipe is made by piercing a solid billet and has no weld seam. Welded pipe (ERW, EFW, SAW) is rolled from plate or strip and welded along its length. B31.3 applies a weld joint efficiency factor to welded pipe, and many specifications require seamless for small bore and high-pressure service.

Link to this answer
FresherName the main butt-weld fittings.

Elbows (90° and 45°, long and short radius), returns, equal and reducing tees, crosses, concentric and eccentric reducers, caps and stub ends, made to ASME B16.9.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is the difference between a long-radius and a short-radius elbow?

A long-radius (LR) elbow has a centre-to-end of 1.5 × NPS (NPS 6 LR = 228.6 mm, or 9 in). A short-radius (SR) elbow has 1.0 × NPS. LR is the default; SR is used only where space is tight, as it has higher pressure drop and stress intensification.

Link to this answer
FresherWhen do you use an eccentric reducer instead of a concentric one?

At pump suctions (flat on top, FOT) so no vapour pocket forms, and on racks or sleepers (flat on bottom, FOB) so the bottom of pipe stays level for supports. Concentric reducers are used on vertical lines and where pockets do not matter.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat are the types of flanges?

Weld neck, slip-on, socket weld, threaded, lap joint (with stub end), blind, plus special types such as orifice, long weld neck, reducing and spectacle blinds. Weld neck is the default for process service because the butt weld can be radiographed and the hub reduces stress.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat are the flange pressure classes in ASME B16.5?

Classes 150, 300, 400, 600, 900, 1500 and 2500 for NPS 1/2 to 24. The class is not a pressure in psi; the allowable pressure depends on the material group and temperature from the B16.5 rating tables.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat are the common flange facings?

Raised face (RF) with spiral wound or sheet gaskets for most service, flat face (FF) to mate with cast iron or non-metallic flanges, and ring type joint (RTJ) for high pressure and temperature. Facings must match on both flanges of a joint.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat gaskets are commonly used on RF flanges?

Spiral wound gaskets (with inner and outer rings) for most hydrocarbon and steam service, compressed fibre or PTFE sheet gaskets for low-pressure utilities, and ring joint gaskets (oval or octagonal) for RTJ facings. ASME B16.20 covers metallic gaskets and B16.21 non-metallic.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhich standard covers flanges above NPS 24, and what is the difference between Series A and B?

ASME B16.47 covers NPS 26 to 60. Series A (from MSS SP-44) flanges are heavier, with fewer, larger bolts; Series B (from API 605) are more compact with more, smaller bolts. The bolt circles differ, so A and B flanges will not bolt together.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is an olet, and when is it used instead of a tee?

An olet (weldolet, sockolet, threadolet, elbolet, latrolet) is a forged branch fitting welded onto the run pipe, with integral reinforcement. It is used for branches much smaller than the header, typically when the branch is less than about half the run size, as given in the branch table.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is branch reinforcement and when is it needed?

When a hole is cut in a header for a stub-in branch, the metal removed must be replaced within a defined zone. B31.3 304.3.3 compares the area required with the excess thickness available in header and branch plus any pad; if it falls short, a reinforcing pad or saddle is added, or a tee or olet is used instead.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a spectacle blind and why is it used?

A figure-8 plate fitted between flanges with a solid blank on one end and a ring spacer on the other. It allows positive isolation for maintenance and is swung or turned between open and closed positions. Above certain sizes and ratings separate spades and spacers are used because of weight.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is the difference between socket weld and threaded small bore piping?

Socket weld joints are fillet-welded and suit most hydrocarbon and higher-pressure small bore service. Threaded joints are used for low-pressure utilities and galvanised water lines; many specifications prohibit threads in hydrocarbon, toxic or cyclic service because of leak risk.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhy must a 1.6 mm gap be left at the bottom of a socket weld joint?

To allow for weld shrinkage and thermal expansion. Without the gap, the pipe bottoms out and the fillet weld can crack. B31.3 and ASME B16.11 practice require about 1.5 mm (1/16 in) before welding.

Link to this answer

Valves

FresherName the common valve types and their main use.

Gate (on/off isolation), globe (throttling and regulation), ball (quick on/off), butterfly (large-bore on/off and throttling), plug (on/off, often for slurries), check (prevents reverse flow), needle (fine control on instruments), and safety/relief valves (overpressure protection).

Link to this answer
FresherWhy is a gate valve not used for throttling?

A partly open gate vibrates and the seat and wedge are eroded by the high-velocity flow, so the valve no longer seals. Gate valves are used fully open or fully closed; globe or control valves are used for throttling.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat types of check valve are there?

Swing check, lift check, ball check, dual plate (wafer) check and piston check. Swing checks suit horizontal and upward vertical flow; dual plate checks are compact and widely used on pump discharges.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is the difference between a safety valve and a relief valve?

A safety valve opens with a sudden pop action and is used for gases and steam. A relief valve opens gradually in proportion to overpressure and is used for liquids. A safety relief valve can do both. All are referred to as PSVs on P&IDs.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhich standards give valve face-to-face dimensions?

ASME B16.10 for flanged and butt-welding end valves; API 609 for butterfly valves; vendor data for others. API 600, 602, 608 and 594 cover design of gate, small forged, ball and check valves.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat do you check when placing a valve in the 3D model?

Operating height and reach, handwheel or gear orientation, clearance for removal and maintenance, access from a platform or grade, stem orientation (not below horizontal on cryogenic or dirty service), flow direction for globes and checks, and that it matches the P&ID sequence.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a double block and bleed arrangement?

Two isolation valves in series with a bleed valve between them, used where positive isolation is needed for maintenance on hazardous service. A single DBB valve combines both seats and the bleed in one body.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhere do you install a control valve, and what goes with it?

Close to grade or a platform for access, with block valves on each side and usually a bypass with a globe valve, plus drain and vent between the blocks. Straight runs and reducer arrangements follow the instrument and vendor requirements, and removal space is kept clear.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat are the layout rules for PSV inlet and outlet piping?

Keep inlet piping short so inlet pressure loss stays below about 3% of set pressure, mount the valve vertically above the protected point, slope the discharge to the flare header with no pockets, and support the outlet for the reaction force. Discharge to atmosphere must go to a safe location.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhy is a check valve placed after a pump and before the discharge block valve?

The check prevents reverse flow and reverse rotation of the pump when it stops; the block valve allows the check to be isolated and removed. Putting the check first means it can be maintained with the pump isolated.

Link to this answer

Materials and specifications

FresherWhat are common pipe materials for carbon steel, low-temperature and stainless service?

ASTM A106 Gr B or API 5L Gr B for carbon steel, A333 Gr 6 for low temperature, A312 TP304/316 (and L grades) for stainless, and A335 P11, P22 or P91 for high-temperature alloy steel.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat are the matching fitting and flange material standards for A106 pipe?

A234 WPB for butt-weld fittings and A105 for forged fittings and flanges. For low-temperature A333 Gr 6 pipe the matches are A420 WPL6 and A350 LF2.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat does the L in 316L mean?

Low carbon (0.03% maximum). It reduces carbide precipitation in the weld heat-affected zone, which improves resistance to intergranular corrosion after welding.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is corrosion allowance, and how is it applied?

Extra wall thickness added to the calculated pressure thickness to allow for corrosion over the plant life, typically 1.5 to 3 mm for carbon steel and zero for stainless. It is set in the piping class and added before choosing the schedule.

Link to this answer
DesignerHow do you calculate pipe wall thickness under B31.3?

Pressure thickness t = P·D / 2(S·E·W + P·Y). Add corrosion and thread or groove allowances to get the required thickness, then divide by (1 − mill tolerance, usually 12.5%) and pick the next schedule up.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a material test certificate (MTC)?

A certificate from the mill showing heat number, chemical composition, mechanical test results and compliance with the specification. EN 10204 3.1 is the usual type; 3.2 adds third-party witness.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is PMI, and when is it required?

Positive material identification: checking alloy composition on site, usually by XRF, to confirm that alloy materials have not been mixed up. It is required for alloy and stainless components, often 100% on critical service.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhen is post-weld heat treatment (PWHT) required?

When the code or the service requires it: B31.3 Table 331.1.1 by material and thickness (for example carbon steel above 19 mm nominal wall), and for services such as caustic, amine, wet H2S and hydrogen. It relieves residual stress and tempers hard zones.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat is NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156?

A standard for materials in sour (H2S-containing) oil and gas service. It limits hardness and specifies material requirements to prevent sulfide stress cracking.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat is the difference between a material spec break and a class break on a P&ID?

A class break shows where the piping class changes, for example from A1A to B1A at a pressure boundary. It must be placed at a flanged joint or a valve, and the higher class usually extends up to and including the isolating valve.

Link to this answer

Piping layout

FresherWhat is a plot plan?

A scaled layout of the plant or unit showing equipment positions, roads, pipe racks, buildings and north. Piping layout uses it to plan routing, rack widths and access.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is a pipe rack?

A steel or concrete structure that carries pipes, cable trays and sometimes air coolers between units and equipment. Large lines go near the columns, utility lines on the top tier or in the middle, and lines that take branches to both sides near the centre.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat clearance is normally kept under a pipe rack?

It is set by the project layout philosophy. Typical values are about 4.5 to 7 m over main roads for vehicles and cranes, 3 to 4 m over secondary access ways, and at least 2.1 m headroom over walkways and platforms.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is BOP and TOS?

BOP is bottom of pipe, used for pipes on racks and sleepers. TOS is top of steel, the elevation of the support beam. On a rack, BOP of an un-shoed pipe equals TOS; with a shoe, BOP is higher by the shoe height.

Link to this answer
DesignerHow do you decide the elevation of pipe rack tiers?

From road clearances, the highest line that must drop to equipment, the space needed for branches and changes of direction between tiers (typically 1.2 to 2 m), plus access for cable trays and air cooler supports.

Link to this answer
DesignerHow do you space pipes on a rack?

By the largest of: OD plus insulation of both pipes plus a clear gap of 25 to 50 mm, flange OD to adjacent pipe, and space for shoes and guides. Flanges are staggered so they do not line up side by side.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat are the main access rules for valves and instruments?

Operating valves within reach from grade or a platform (about 0.75 to 1.5 m above the floor), chain wheels only where agreed, gauges readable from the operating position, and every item needing maintenance reachable with clear access paths and escape routes.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a pocket, and why should it be avoided?

A low point where liquid collects (in gas lines) or a high point where gas collects (in liquid lines). Pockets cause slugging, corrosion and draining problems. Where they cannot be avoided, drains and vents are added; some lines (relief, slurry) must have no pockets at all.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhere do you place high-point vents and low-point drains?

At every high point of liquid lines and every low point of all lines that need draining for hydrotest or maintenance. Process vents and drains are permanent with valves; hydrotest-only vents and drains may be plugged.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a slope, and which lines need it?

A fall of the line in the flow direction, such as 1:100 or 1:200, given on the P&ID. Flare headers, relief discharges, blowdown, condensate and gravity drains need it so liquid drains freely.

Link to this answer
SeniorHow do you route a line from the rack down to a pump?

Drop from the rack to a level above the pump, keep the suction short with minimum fittings, use an eccentric reducer FOT and a straight length before the nozzle as the vendor requires, and support the piping independently so loads on the nozzle stay within allowable values.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat are the main safety distances considered in layout?

Spacing between fired heaters and hydrocarbon equipment, between process units and control rooms, from storage tanks, flare radiation zones, and fire-fighting access, as set by standards such as OISD (India), NFPA, API and client guidelines.

Link to this answer
SeniorHow do you plan maintenance and removal space?

Reserve clear areas for exchanger bundle pulls, pump and motor removal, control valve and PSV removal, crane and truck access, and dropout zones on platforms. They are shown in the model as reserved volumes and checked at model reviews.

Link to this answer

Equipment piping

FresherWhat is a nozzle orientation drawing?

A drawing of a vessel or column showing the angular position, elevation and projection of each nozzle, platforms, ladders and davits. Piping prepares it to suit layout, and the vessel vendor builds to it.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat are the main points for pump suction piping?

Short and direct, no high points or pockets, eccentric reducer flat on top, straight length before the nozzle (often 3 to 5 D or as the vendor specifies), a temporary strainer for start-up, and no elbows in the plane of the impeller close to the nozzle on double-suction pumps.

Link to this answer
DesignerHow is piping arranged around a shell and tube heat exchanger?

Keep the channel end clear for bundle pulling, put removable spools at channel nozzles, keep shell-side piping clear of the bundle removal area, provide access to the channel cover and bolting, and stack exchangers so piping is simple.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat are the main points for column (tower) piping?

Lines run down the column on the pipe-way side at a fixed distance from the shell, supported and guided from clips on the column, with manholes, platforms and ladders on the access side. Reflux, feed and overhead lines are routed to keep pockets and loads within limits.

Link to this answer
DesignerHow do you lay out piping around a storage tank?

Use flexible connections or enough loop to absorb tank settlement and bulging, keep the first support off the tank foundation, provide drain and inlet arrangements per the vendor and API 650, and keep piping out of the bund area where possible.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is checked at compressor piping?

Nozzle loads to API 617 or 618, vibration (pulsation study for reciprocating compressors), suction strainers, knockout drums before the suction, removal space for the casing and overhead crane access, and supports on independent foundations.

Link to this answer
SeniorHow do you check nozzle loads on pumps?

The stress engineer calculates forces and moments at each nozzle from the stress model and compares them with API 610 Table 5 (or the vendor allowable values). If they are exceeded, the piping is rerouted, given more flexibility or supported differently.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat does a piping engineer do with vendor drawings?

Check nozzle sizes, ratings, facings and positions against the model and datasheets, pass comments to the vendor, and update the model and isometrics when the final certified drawing arrives. Late vendor data is a common cause of rework.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat is a fired heater coil crossover, and what is special about its piping?

The piping between convection and radiant sections or outside the heater box. It runs hot, often in alloy steel, so it needs flexibility, spring supports and careful terminal-point loads agreed with the heater vendor.

Link to this answer

Supports and stress

FresherWhat is the purpose of a pipe support?

To carry the weight of pipe, fluid, insulation and components, keep stresses and sag within limits, control movement from thermal expansion, and stop vibration. Supports also protect equipment nozzles from loads.

Link to this answer
FresherName the common support types.

Rest (shoe), guide, line stop, anchor, U-bolt, clamp, hanger (rod), spring (variable or constant), dummy leg, trunnion, and snubber or damper for dynamic loads.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is the difference between a guide and a line stop?

A guide stops movement across the pipe (laterally) and allows axial sliding. A line stop stops axial movement in one or both directions. An anchor stops movement in all directions.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhen is a spring support used?

When a line moves vertically because of thermal expansion and a rigid support would lose contact or overload the nozzle. Variable springs suit small movements; constant springs suit large movements where load change must be minimal.

Link to this answer
DesignerHow are support spans decided?

From standard span tables for each size and service (often based on B31.1 Table 121.5 or company tables), checked for deflection (typically 2.5 to 5 mm) and stress, and reduced at concentrated loads such as valves and flanges.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhich lines are sent for formal stress analysis?

Lines meeting the project stress criticality list: for example high and low temperature lines, lines connected to rotating equipment, large-bore lines, relief and flare lines, high-pressure lines, and lines with vibration or slug flow.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat does a stress engineer check in CAESAR II?

Sustained, expansion and occasional stresses against B31.3 limits, nozzle loads against equipment allowables, flange leakage, support loads and displacements, and frictional effects. Results drive support types and routing changes.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat is an expansion loop, and how is it sized?

A U-shaped offset added to a long straight line so thermal growth is taken by bending instead of by large anchor loads. Its leg length comes from the expansion to absorb and the allowable stress, often checked with the guided-cantilever approach and confirmed by stress analysis.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat is the difference between primary and secondary stress?

Primary stresses come from sustained loads such as pressure and weight and are not self-limiting. Secondary stresses come from restrained thermal expansion and are self-limiting, so B31.3 allows a higher displacement stress range.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat is a dummy leg, and what are its checks?

A pipe welded to an elbow to carry the line down to a support. Check the local stress in the elbow, add a weep hole, keep the leg size per the standard, and check for differential expansion between the line and the steel.

Link to this answer

Isometrics, drawings and 3D modelling

FresherWhat is a piping isometric?

A drawing of one line in isometric projection, not to scale, with all dimensions, elevations, components, welds, supports and a bill of materials. It is used for fabrication, erection, testing and records.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is a GA (general arrangement) drawing?

A plan and section drawing of an area showing the routing of pipes, equipment, structures and supports, with key dimensions and elevations. With 3D models, GAs are extracted from the model.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat 3D modelling tools are used in piping design?

Hexagon Smart 3D (S3D), AVEVA E3D (formerly PDMS), AutoCAD Plant 3D and others. Isometrics are generated with Isogen or a similar tool, and the model is reviewed with Navisworks or the viewer of the tool.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat is a model review?

Formal reviews of the 3D model at about 30%, 60% and 90% completion with process, operations, safety, maintenance, instrument, electrical and civil to check layout, access and safety. Comments are recorded and closed in the model.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a clash check?

An automatic or manual check in the 3D model for physical interference between pipes, steel, equipment, cable trays and reserved volumes. Clashes are reviewed and resolved by the disciplines involved before isometrics are issued.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat information goes on an isometric title block?

Line number, size, class, fluid, design and operating pressure and temperature, test pressure and medium, insulation and tracing, painting, PWHT, NDE percentage, P&ID and GA references and the revision.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a spool, and how is it marked?

A shop-fabricated piece of piping between field joints, small enough to transport and erect. Spools are numbered on the isometric and marked for tracking through fabrication, painting and erection.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is the difference between a shop weld and a field weld?

A shop weld is made in the fabrication shop under controlled conditions. A field weld is made on site to join spools during erection. Field fit welds (FFW) are left long to be cut to fit on site.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is an MTO, and how is it taken?

A material take-off lists pipe lengths, fittings, flanges, valves, gaskets and bolts for procurement. Early MTOs are estimated from P&IDs and the plot plan; later MTOs come from the 3D model and isometrics with surplus allowances.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat are the 30%, 60% and 90% model stages?

30%: equipment, main racks and major lines; 60%: most lines routed, supports and platforms, ready for stress and isometric start; 90%: everything modelled including small bore, ready for issue for construction. Percentages and contents vary by company.

Link to this answer
SeniorHow do you control design changes after isometrics are issued?

Through revision control: changes are clouded with a new revision, logged against an approved change, and communicated to fabrication and site. Late changes need impact checks on material, stress and supports.

Link to this answer

Testing, inspection and site

FresherWhat is a hydrostatic test?

A pressure test with water at 1.5 times the design pressure (B31.3, adjusted for temperature), held long enough to inspect every joint for leaks. It proves the strength and tightness of the piping before commissioning.

Link to this answer
FresherWhen is a pneumatic test used instead of hydrotest?

When water cannot be used, for example lines that cannot be dried or supported for water weight, or cryogenic and some gas services. B31.3 sets the pneumatic test at 1.1 times the design pressure, with extra safety precautions because stored energy is much higher.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat are the common NDE methods for welds?

Visual inspection, radiography (RT), ultrasonic testing (UT), magnetic particle (MT) and dye penetrant (PT). The extent (for example 5%, 10% or 100% RT) depends on the fluid service and piping class.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is a test pack?

A package of isometrics, P&IDs and records for one test circuit: the test limits, test pressure and medium, vents and drains, items to remove or isolate, and sign-offs. It is used for pressure testing and handover.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat must be removed or isolated before hydrotest?

Instruments, control valves, PSVs, rotating equipment, expansion joints and anything not rated for the test pressure. Spring supports are locked, and temporary supports are added where the water weight needs them.

Link to this answer
DesignerWhat is flushing and why is it done?

Cleaning the inside of the piping with water, air or steam after erection to remove debris, scale and weld spatter before start-up. Lube oil and compressor suction lines need special cleaning such as pickling and oil flushing.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat is the site role of a piping engineer during construction?

Answering queries from site, approving field changes and redlines, checking support installation and spring settings, resolving clashes found during erection, and updating as-built records.

Link to this answer
SeniorWhat are as-built drawings?

Drawings updated with the changes made during construction so they show what was actually installed. They are handed over to the owner for operation and maintenance.

Projects and career

FresherWhat are the phases of an EPC project?

Feasibility, FEED (front-end engineering design), detailed engineering, procurement, construction, pre-commissioning and commissioning, then handover. Piping works in all engineering phases and supports site.

Link to this answer
FresherWhat does a piping designer do day to day?

Routes lines in the 3D model, extracts and checks isometrics, places supports, resolves clashes, checks vendor drawings, prepares equipment layouts and nozzle orientations, and attends model reviews and coordination meetings.

FresherWhat should a fresher learn first for a piping design job?

Read P&IDs and piping classes, understand pipes, fittings, flanges and valves, learn isometric drafting and one 3D tool (S3D or E3D), and practise layout basics such as pump and exchanger piping. Being able to explain your choices matters more than knowing every number.

Link to this answer
DesignerHow does piping coordinate with other disciplines?

Process for P&IDs and line data, mechanical for equipment and vendor data, civil and structural for foundations, racks and platforms, instrumentation for instrument locations and connections, electrical for cable trays and lighting, and stress for supports and routing.

Link to this answer
SeniorHow would you handle a late vendor change that affects many lines?

Assess the impact quickly in the model, raise a change notice with cost and schedule impact, agree priorities with the lead and project, update the model, stress and isometrics in order of construction need, and communicate clearly to site and fabrication.
